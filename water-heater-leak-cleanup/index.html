Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Santa Fe: What to Know

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

After extracting the water, our Santa Fe crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • The volume of wet stored items that must be dried, moved or disposed of.
  • The length of time the tank went on refilling until someone closed the supply valve.
  • The location of the heater, since a garage leak costs less than one in an interior closet or attic.
  • Condition of the stand or subfloor below the heater, which may need rebuilding before the replacement is set.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
  • The drain valve at the bottom of the tank cracking, loosening or failing to shut fully.
  • Drain pans beneath closet or attic units that rust through or clog and then spill over.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve that opens or fails and discharges water.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Heater Closet Drywall

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Stored Belongings

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Attic and Ceilings

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Platform and Subfloor

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

How We Fix It

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Extract the Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

We go over the wall bases, the platform, any ceiling underneath and the adjoining rooms with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which shows how far the wet area extends.

3

Remove Wet Material

Soaked insulation, crumbling drywall and damaged baseboards are taken out. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

4

Dry and Monitor

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.
