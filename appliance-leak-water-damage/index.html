Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Santa Fe

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Cleanup is different for each of them.

For homeowners in Santa Fe, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Mop Up What You CanGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Leave the Door ShutA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Document What FailedPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • Inlet valves that stick open, so the dishwasher or washer keeps taking in water past the fill level.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • Ice maker lines, copper or plastic, that crack, kink or come loose where they connect.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Mold Behind Appliances

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Wall Bases and Trim

Drywall edges and baseboards pull up water that stands on a kitchen or laundry room floor. The wall behind the appliance is often the wettest because nobody could see it.

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

In a home with an upstairs laundry, leak water passes through the floor and pools on the ceiling underneath. Drywall there sags and stains while the insulation over it holds water.

Adjoining Rooms

A washer hose can release water for hours. It passes under walls into hallways, closets and bedrooms, and soaks carpet and pad well away from the laundry room.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • Whether mold has started and has to be removed before the area can be dried.
  • Type of water, since gray discharge requires sanitizing and clean supply water does not.
  • Which appliance leaked and whether it was a fast flood or a slow seep.
  • How many rooms the water reached, including any ceiling below an upstairs laundry.

What We Do

Our Appliance Leak Water Damage Process

1

Pull and Inspect

After making sure the supply is shut and moving the disconnected appliance, our crew follows the water with meters and a thermal camera into nearby walls, under flooring and beneath cabinets.

2

Extract the Water

We extract standing water out of carpet and off hard floors. To reach water caught below cabinets or under floating floors, our crew pulls toe kicks or takes up boards.

3

Dry in Place

We set air movers and dehumidifiers and, where possible, dry cabinets and hardwood in place with directed airflow so they do not have to be torn out.

4

Sanitize and Take Final Readings

Surfaces touched by washer or dishwasher discharge get antimicrobial treatment. We take final moisture readings before the appliance goes back so it is not set on a damp floor.
